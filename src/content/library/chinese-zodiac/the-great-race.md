## The invitation

Long ago, the Jade Emperor, ruler of the heavens, wanted a way to measure time. He announced a race: the first twelve animals to cross the great river and reach his palace would each have a year named after them, in the order they arrived.

## The crossing

- The **Rat** and the **Cat** could not swim, so they asked the kind **Ox** for a ride. Halfway across, the Rat pushed the Cat into the water. As the Ox reached the bank, the Rat jumped ahead and came **first**. The Ox came **second**.
- The powerful **Tiger** fought the currents and came **third**.
- The **Rabbit** hopped across on stones and floated the last stretch on a log, coming **fourth**.
- The **Dragon**, who could have flown, stopped to bring rain to a thirsty village and to blow the Rabbit's log ashore. It came **fifth**.
- The **Horse** was about to finish when the **Snake**, coiled around its hoof, slid out and startled it. The Snake came **sixth** and the Horse **seventh**.
- The **Goat**, **Monkey** and **Rooster** worked together on a raft, arriving **eighth**, **ninth** and **tenth**.
- The **Dog** stopped to play in the water and came **eleventh**.
- The **Pig** stopped for a meal and a nap, and came **twelfth**.

## Why there is no Cat (except in Vietnam)

The Cat, pushed into the river by the Rat, arrived too late. Some say this is why cats and rats are enemies to this day. In Vietnam, however, the Cat takes the Rabbit's place in the zodiac.

## The meaning behind the story

Every animal's way of crossing hints at its character: the Rat's cunning, the Ox's kindness, the Dragon's generosity, the Pig's love of comfort. Each animal page in the Library starts with its part of the story.
