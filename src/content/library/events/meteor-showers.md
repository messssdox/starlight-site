## What a shooting star is

A meteor, or shooting star, is a speck of dust, often no bigger than a grain of sand, burning up as it hits our atmosphere at tens of kilometres per second. When Earth passes through a stream of dust left behind by a comet, we see many meteors that all seem to come from one point in the sky, called the radiant. The shower is named after the constellation where the radiant lies.

## The best showers of the year

| Shower | Peak | At best |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quadrantids | about 3-4 January | up to 100 an hour, but a short peak |
| Lyrids | about 22 April | around 18 an hour |
| Eta Aquariids | about 5-6 May | best from the southern hemisphere |
| Perseids | about 12-13 August | up to 100 an hour, warm summer nights |
| Orionids | about 21 October | around 20 an hour, from Halley's Comet |
| Leonids | about 17 November | around 15 an hour, occasional storms |
| Geminids | about 13-14 December | up to 120 an hour, the year's best |

Rates are for a perfectly dark sky; from a town you will see fewer. A bright Moon can wash out all but the brightest meteors.

## How to watch

- Find somewhere dark, away from street lights.
- Give your eyes 20 minutes to adjust, and don't look at your phone.
- Lie back and look at a wide area of sky, not just the radiant.
- After midnight is usually best.
- No telescope needed: just your eyes, a blanket and patience.
