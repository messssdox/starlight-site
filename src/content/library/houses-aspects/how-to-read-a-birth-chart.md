## The chart is a map of the sky

A birth chart (or natal chart) is a map of the sky at the moment and place you were born, drawn as a wheel. The outer ring shows the twelve signs; the inner sections are the twelve houses; the symbols are the planets; and the lines across the middle are the aspects.

## A five-step way in

1. **Start with your big three.** Find your Sun, Moon and Rising sign. These give the outline of your personality. See [Your big three](/library/sun-moon-rising/your-big-three).
2. **Look at the balance of elements.** Count how many planets are in Fire, Earth, Air and Water signs. A lot of one element is a strong theme; a missing element is something you may seek in others. See [Fire, Earth, Air and Water](/library/elements/fire-earth-air-and-water).
3. **Notice busy houses.** Several planets in one house show an area of life that matters a great deal to you. See [The twelve houses](/library/houses-aspects/the-twelve-houses).
4. **Find the closest aspects.** The tightest angles between planets are the loudest conversations in your chart. See [The major aspects](/library/houses-aspects/the-major-aspects).
5. **Put it into a sentence.** Planet (what) + sign (how) + house (where). "My Mars (drive) is in Capricorn (patient and ambitious) in the tenth house (career)."

## Don't try to read it all at once

Even professional astrologers take a chart one piece at a time. Start with what interests you, and let the rest unfold. Starlight's First Timers reading explains each part as it goes.
