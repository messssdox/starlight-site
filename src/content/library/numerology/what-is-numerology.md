## An old idea

People have found meaning in numbers for thousands of years. The Greek philosopher Pythagoras, around 500 BCE, taught that numbers are the building blocks of the universe, and the system most people use today carries his name. Modern numerology as we know it took shape in the early twentieth century.

## How it works

Numerology reduces your birth date and your name to single numbers from 1 to 9 by adding digits together. So 28 becomes 2 + 8 = 10, and 10 becomes 1 + 0 = 1. The exceptions are the [master numbers](/library/numerology/master-numbers) 11, 22 and 33, which are kept as they are.

## Where traditions differ

Chaldean numerology, which is older, uses different letter values (1 to 8) and gives more weight to the name you use every day. Some numerologists also add up every digit of the birth date in one long line instead of reducing the day, month and year separately; that can occasionally hide a master number. Starlight uses the Pythagorean letters and reduces day, month and year separately, and shows every step.
