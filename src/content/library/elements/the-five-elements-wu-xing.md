## Five phases, not five substances

The Chinese term Wu Xing is often translated as "five elements", but "five phases" or "five movements" is closer. They describe how energy changes, the way the seasons turn from one to the next.

| Element | Season | Direction | Colour | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood 木 | Spring | East | Green | growth, kindness, vision |
| Fire 火 | Summer | South | Red | warmth, passion, joy |
| Earth 土 | Turn of each season | Centre | Yellow | balance, care, stability |
| Metal 金 | Autumn | West | White | clarity, focus, letting go |
| Water 水 | Winter | North | Black and blue | wisdom, rest, flow |

## The feeding cycle

Each element nourishes the next:

**Wood** feeds **Fire** (wood burns) → **Fire** makes **Earth** (ash) → **Earth** bears **Metal** (ore in the ground) → **Metal** carries **Water** (condensation on cold metal) → **Water** nourishes **Wood** (plants drink).

## The controlling cycle

Each element also keeps another in check:

**Wood** breaks up **Earth** (roots) · **Earth** dams **Water** · **Water** puts out **Fire** · **Fire** melts **Metal** · **Metal** cuts **Wood**.

Neither cycle is good or bad. Feeding without control runs wild; control without feeding withers. Balance needs both.

## On the Starlight wheel

The four seasons sit around the edge of the wheel: Water (winter) covers the Pig, Rat and Ox; Wood (spring) the Tiger, Rabbit and Dragon; Fire (summer) the Snake, Horse and Goat; Metal (autumn) the Monkey, Rooster and Dog. Earth has no season of its own; it sits at the turn of each season, in the Ox, Dragon, Goat and Dog.
